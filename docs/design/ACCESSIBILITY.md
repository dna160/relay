# Accessibility

> Target: WCAG 2.2 AA. Every ratio in this document was computed, not asserted.
> The method is the WCAG 2.x relative-luminance formula
> (`0.2126R + 0.7152G + 0.0722B` on linearised sRGB, `(L1+0.05)/(L2+0.05)`),
> applied to the exact hex values in `src/app/globals.css`.
>
> If you change a token, recompute this file. A contrast claim that is not a
> measurement is a lie with a table around it.

### 1.1 Tokens that had to be adjusted

Two changes, both stated rather than smoothed over. **Both have since been
folded into the body of `docs/DESIGN-SYSTEM.md`** (round 2, directive U1), so
the design system no longer publishes a failing value that a later reader would
re-derive. What follows is the working.

**`--muted` changed from `#6B7168` to `#60665D`.** The published value measured
**4.139:1** on `--paper` and failed AA. `--muted` carries real text — due dates,
engagement subtitles, hints, the size and hash columns of a version stack — so a
non-text exemption does not apply. The replacement holds the same hue
(hsl 100°, 4% saturation) and drops lightness by ~11%, measuring **4.874:1**.

**`--rule-strong` #808978 / #616D77 added.** `--rule` measures 1.40:1 and cannot
be the sole boundary of a control under WCAG 1.4.11 (3:1 for user-interface
components). Rather than darken `--rule` — which would coarsen every hairline in
a product built out of hairlines — the token was split. `--rule` stays a
decorative hairline: table rules, dividers between version rows, the edge of a
card that already has a `--paper-2` ground distinguishing it. `--rule-strong` is
what an input, a button outline, or a section underline is drawn with, and it
measures 3.01:1 / 3.39:1. No control in the primitive layer uses `--rule` as its
only boundary.

### 1.2 Dark-mode lift, exactly

DESIGN-SYSTEM.md originally specified "possession hues lighten by 18%". Applied
as +18 HSL lightness points and then measured against **`--paper-2`**, which is
the ground cards actually sit on:

| Token | +18 result | vs dark `--paper` | vs dark `--paper-2` | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `--agency` | #399081 | 4.70 | **4.23** | fails on cards |
| `--client` | #8487C8 | 5.38 | 4.84 | passes, shipped |
| `--breach` | #E1443D | **4.37** | **3.93** | fails both |
| `--muted` | #999F96 | 6.64 | 5.98 | passes, shipped |

So: `--client` and `--muted` ship at +18. `--agency` ships at **+20** (#499D8F,
the smallest lift clearing 4.5:1 on `--paper-2`) and `--breach` at **+23**
(#E45953). Hue and saturation are untouched in all four, so the relationship
between them — pine cooler and greener, indigo warmer and bluer, and both well
clear of the red — survives the lift.

The shipped `--agency` in dark mode is generated by the same OKLCH clamp the
white-label hook goes through (see §2), which lands it at OKLCH lightness 0.64.

## 2. White-label cannot break contrast

A tenant sets one property, `--brand-agency`. `--agency` then resolves down one
of two paths, and **only the tenant path is clamped**:

```css
/* the tenant branch — declared only inside @supports, and note that
   var(--brand-agency) carries NO fallback */
/* light */ --agency-tenant: oklch(from var(--brand-agency) clamp(0.20, l, 0.50) clamp(0, c, 0.12) h);
/* dark  */ --agency-tenant: oklch(from var(--brand-agency) clamp(0.64, l, 0.90) clamp(0, calc(c*1.6), 0.12) h);

/* the selection. --brand-agency is deliberately undeclared, so with no tenant
   --agency-tenant is invalid at computed-value time and the literal wins. */
--agency: var(--agency-tenant, #1F4E46);   /* light */
--agency: var(--agency-tenant, #499D8F);   /* dark  */
```

The default never goes through the clamp, and that is load-bearing rather than
tidy. In round 2 it did — via `var(--brand-agency, #1f4e46)` — and the dark
expression's `c * 1.6` chroma lift re-lifted a colour that was already lifted,
so the browser painted **rgb(0, 163, 144)** while this document, `DESIGN-SYSTEM.md`
and `a11y-contract.ts` all published `#499D8F`. It measured 5.690:1 on `--paper`
against the recorded 5.571:1 — legible, and therefore invisible to every ratio
assertion in this file. A contrast table can be right about the wrong colour.
`A11Y-ASSERTIONS.md` §4 now asserts the untenanted `--agency` resolves to the
exact published hex, per mode, which is the only shape of test that catches it.

The two bands were chosen by exhaustive search over the sRGB gamut — every hue
at 2° steps, every chroma at 0.005 steps — for the worst case at each lightness:

- **Light ceiling 0.50.** At OKLCH L = 0.50 the brightest in-gamut colour of any
  hue has relative luminance 0.1357. Against `--paper` (Y = 0.8166) that is
  **4.67:1**. At L = 0.51 the worst case is 0.1438, giving 4.43:1 — which is why
  the ceiling is 0.50 and not 0.55.
- **Dark floor 0.64.** At OKLCH L = 0.64 with chroma capped at 0.12, the darkest
  in-gamut colour of any hue has luminance 0.2434. Against dark `--paper-2`
  (Y = 0.0148) that is **4.50:1**. At L = 0.63 the worst case gives 4.44:1.

So **no value a tenant can supply produces an `--agency` below 4.5:1 on either
ground, in either mode.** They can change the hue; they cannot make possession
illegible.

`--on-hue` is `--paper` in both modes, which is not a coincidence: ground and
possession hue sit at opposite ends of the lightness axis, and both invert
together. Measured on the shipped tokens:

| Mode | Text | Fill | Ratio |
|---|---|---|---|
| Light | `--on-hue` #E8EAE5 | `--agency` #1F4E46 | 7.75 |
| Light | `--on-hue` #E8EAE5 | `--client` #4A4FA6 | 5.85 |
| Light | `--on-hue` #E8EAE5 | `--breach` #A8201A | 6.00 |
| Dark | `--on-hue` #14171A | `--agency` #499D8F | 5.57 |
| Dark | `--on-hue` #14171A | `--client` #8487C8 | 5.38 |
| Dark | `--on-hue` #14171A | `--breach` #E45953 | 5.01 |

Across the full clamped white-label band the worst case for `--on-hue` on an
`--agency` fill is **4.67:1** (light, L = 0.50) and **5.02:1** (dark, L = 0.64).

Browsers without relative colour syntax fall back to the Relay default and get
**no white-label at all** — the whole tenant branch lives inside the `@supports`
block, so neither `--agency-tenant` nor the `var()` that selects it is ever
declared. There is deliberately no code path in which an unclamped tenant colour
reaches the page.

Verified in-browser across all four combinations of light/dark x default/tenant,
plus the seven `HOSTILE_BRAND_VALUES`:

| Mode | Tenant | Painted `--agency` | on `--paper` | on `--paper-2` |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Light | none | **#1F4E46** | 7.750 | 8.430 |
| Light | worst of seven (`#00FF00`) | #347430 | 4.70 | 5.11 |
| Dark | none | **#499D8F** | 5.571 | 5.016 |
| Dark | worst of seven (`#000000`) | #8C8C8C | 5.35 | 4.82 |

The two untenanted rows match `TOKENS` in `a11y-contract.ts` to three decimal
places, and the derived `--tint-agency` resolves to `#D9DFDC` / `#223032` — the
recorded values — rather than to a mix of a drifted hue.

`--client`, `--breach` and every neutral are literals declared `!important` on
both `:root` and `[data-relay-root]`, which is the element the tenant hook lives
on — an `!important` author declaration beats a normal inline one on the same
element. A tenant cannot theme away a warning.

## 3. Colour is never the only channel

Contrast is not the same as comprehension. Pine and indigo differ strongly in
hue but, in dark mode, sit at nearly the same luminance (5.57 vs 5.38 on
`--paper`) — which is exactly the condition a colour-blind reader cannot resolve.

So the possession hue is **always redundant**:

- `PossessionBar` always renders its mono label — `agency · 6d` / `client · 6d`.
  The word is the primary channel; the hue is the glanceable one.
- The bar carries `role="img"` with an `aria-label` that says the same thing in
  a sentence.
- The state `Chip` in its quiet variant sets its label in `--ink`, not in the
  hue, so a chip is legible with the colour channel entirely removed.
- `--breach` never appears without the number that justifies it (`4/2`) and a
  screen-reader label that says "over contract".

A reasonable test: render the board in greyscale. Nothing should become
ambiguous. If it does, a hue is doing work a word should be doing.

### Chip tint legibility, measured

| Mode | Label | Ground | Ratio |
|---|---|---|---|
| Light | `--ink` | `--tint-agency` #D9DFDC | 13.31 |
| Light | `--ink` | `--tint-client` #DEDFE7 | 13.55 |
| Light | `--ink` | `--tint-breach` #E9DAD6 | 13.25 |
| Light | `--ink` | `--tint-neutral` #D7D9D6 | 12.67 |
| Dark | `--ink` | `--tint-agency` #223032 | 11.28 |
| Dark | `--ink` | `--tint-client` #292D39 | 11.34 |
| Dark | `--ink` | `--tint-breach` #35282B | 11.63 |
| Dark | `--ink` | `--tint-neutral` #35393C | 9.62 |

(The hue set *on* its own tint would measure 3.93–4.23 in dark mode. That is why
the quiet chip's label is `--ink` and the hue lives in the tint and the 2px
leading rule instead.)

`--tint-neutral` is the fourth of the set and it is a fix, not a completeness
exercise. A chip carrying no possession has no hue to tint with, so it was
painted `--paper-2` — which *is* a card's ground, so a neutral chip on a card
had no boundary at all and `CardTile` had to override the primitive to get one
back. Same 12% construction, mixed from `--ink`: the chip keeps a ground of its
own on `--paper` and `--paper-2` alike, and nothing at a call site has to know
which one it is standing on. A `ground` prop was the alternative and was
rejected on the same grounds as a `motion-reduce:` variant — a call-site opt-in
is a thing the next call site forgets.

## 4. Focus

### The ring

```css
outline: 2px solid var(--focus);   /* --focus = --ink */
outline-offset: 2px;
```

Applied globally in `globals.css` §6 to `a, button, input, textarea, select,
summary, [tabindex]` on `:focus-visible`. Nothing in this codebase may set
`outline: none` without an equally visible replacement, and the primitive layer
never does.

`--focus` is neutral by design. A possession-coloured ring would put focus and
possession on the same channel, and a focused agency card would be
indistinguishable from a client card.

**WCAG 2.4.11 (Focus Not Obscured) and 2.4.13 (Focus Appearance)**: the ring is
2px, unbroken, and encloses the whole control. The 2px *offset* is load-bearing
— it means the colours adjacent to the ring on both sides are the page ground,
not the control's fill:

| Mode | Ring | Adjacent colour | Ratio |
|---|---|---|---|
| Light | `--ink` #14171A | `--paper` #E8EAE5 | 14.85 |
| Light | `--ink` #14171A | `--paper-2` #F2F3F0 | 16.15 |
| Dark | `--ink` #E8EAE5 | `--paper` #14171A | 14.85 |
| Dark | `--ink` #E8EAE5 | `--paper-2` #1D2125 | 13.37 |

Without the offset, an ink ring directly against an `--agency` fill would
measure 1.92:1 and fail. The offset is not decoration; do not remove it.

Sticky surfaces (`WrapSlate` at `z-slate`, the mobile `DecisionBar` at
`bottom-0`) must not cover a focused control. Both reserve their own height via
`scroll-padding-top` / `scroll-padding-bottom` on the scroll container, set to
the slate and bar heights.

### Focus order

Order follows DOM order everywhere. No `tabindex` above 0 exists in this
codebase.

**Client card view** — the order an uninvested user on a phone needs:

1. Skip link → "Skip to the decision" (visually hidden until focused). This is
   the single most valuable accessibility affordance in the product: a keyboard
   or screen-reader user should not traverse a version stack to reach the two
   buttons they came for.
2. `WrapSlate` — the records are not focusable; `Export` is.
3. Card title (heading, not focusable; reachable by heading navigation).
4. Current version download.
5. `Show earlier versions` disclosure.
6. `DecisionBar`: radio group (one tab stop, arrow keys move within it), then
   the note Textarea if mounted, then Cancel, then Submit.

**Agency board** — lane by lane, left to right, and within a lane: the lane's
`Add` control comes **after** its cards, not before, so tabbing through the board
is tabbing through work rather than through affordances.

**Dialog** — native `<dialog>` + `showModal()`, so focus containment, page
inertness and Escape come from the platform. On open, focus lands on the first
focusable element in the panel; on close, the browser returns it to the invoker.
`dismissible={false}` suppresses Escape via `onCancel` + `preventDefault`, which
is used for the purge confirmation and nothing else.

## 5. The decision bar's required-note affordance

This is the one place in the product where a disabled control is unavoidable —
`note` is required when the decision is `changes_requested`, enforced in the
domain and by a CHECK constraint — so it gets the full treatment.

Four rules, all required:

1. **The reason is always visible.** A `text-12 text-muted` line sits beside the
   disabled submit reading "Add a note to send this back." (or "Choose Approve
   or Request changes." before a choice is made). A disabled button with no
   explanation is how you lose a client to email.
2. **The reason is announced.** That line has `aria-live="polite"` and its id is
   the submit button's `aria-describedby`, so a reader landing on the disabled
   button hears the reason without hunting for it.
3. **The requirement is visible before it bites.** The Textarea mounts the moment
   "Request changes" is chosen — not on failed submit — and its label renders a
   visible "(required)" in `--muted` via the `Field` primitive.
4. **Nothing moves.** Hint and error occupy the same slot with a `min-h-4`
   reservation, so the layout does not shift under a thumb when validation
   fires.

The threshold is one non-whitespace character. Do not invent a minimum length.

A note on the disabled state itself: `disabled` (not `aria-disabled`) is used,
so the button is removed from the tab order. That is correct here because the
control immediately preceding it — the Textarea — is the thing that unblocks it,
and it is one shift-tab away with the reason already announced.

## 6. `--breach`, exhaustively

`--breach` appears in exactly one situation in the entire product:

> **`roundsUsed > contractedRounds` on a card** — rendered as the rounds counter
> in `CardTile` and `AttentionRow` (`4/2`), with the accessible label "over
> contract".

That is the list. Everything else that a conventional interface would paint red
uses **weight and `--ink`** instead:

| Situation | Treatment |
|---|---|
| Overdue due date | `text-ink font-semibold` |
| Long possession (> 7 days) | `text-ink font-semibold` |
| Purge countdown at any distance | `text-ink font-semibold`, plus surface area |
| Form validation error | `text-ink font-semibold`, 3px `--ink` leading bar on the field, `role="alert"` |
| Server error | `text-ink font-semibold`, `role="alert"` |
| Destructive confirmation (purge now) | `quiet` Button inside a `dismissible={false}` Dialog that states the consequence |
| Failed upload (`UploadDock`, any of the four steps) | `text-ink font-semibold` phase line with `role="alert"`, plus an `--ink` row edge. A failed upload is not a breached commitment. |
| Dropped event stream (`StreamStatus` degraded / offline) | The mono label changes from a word to a timestamp, the dot goes darker rather than redder, and past 30 minutes a banner with an `--ink` leading bar. |

This is a deliberate deviation from the convention that errors are red, taken
because DESIGN-SYSTEM.md reserves the colour absolutely and the reservation is
only worth anything if it is absolute. Accessibility is unaffected: an error is
identified by weight, by an alert role, by a shape change on the field, and by
its text — never by colour, which WCAG 1.4.1 requires anyway.

## 7. Reduced motion

**Round 3 replaced the one-crossfade motion budget with a motion system. The
mechanism did not change, and this section is the proof that it did not.**

Full specification: `docs/design/MOTION.md`. What matters here is the single
guarantee.

Every duration in Relay is an integer number of beats, written as a `calc()`
over one token and never as a literal:

```css
--dur-beat: 60ms;
--time-chip: calc(var(--dur-beat) * 2);   /* the 120ms crossfade, unchanged */
--time-attach: calc(var(--time-strike) + var(--time-seat));  /* five beats */
```

So reduced motion is still one declaration, and it still cannot be forgotten:

```css
@media (prefers-reduced-motion: reduce) {
  :root, [data-relay-root] {
    --dur-beat: 0ms;
    --dist-strike: 0px;
    --dist-seat: 0px;
    --dist-nudge: 0px;
    --scale-stamp: 1;
    --tilt-strike: 0deg;
  }
}
```

Zero the beat and every multiple collapses arithmetically. **The proof is still
"one token, one assertion"** no matter how many named durations sit on top of
it, which is why `tests/unit/a11y-source.spec.ts` may keep asserting that
exactly one `--dur-*` token exists in `globals.css`. That assertion was read in
round 1 as a budget on how much motion there is. It is not — it is a proof that
there is one switch, and it survives round 3 unrelaxed.

The amplitudes are zeroed alongside the beat as belt and braces: a transition
that somehow fires with a literal duration still has nowhere to travel.

**Under reduce the interface is deliberate, not frozen, and the reason is
structural.** Every keyframe in the product resolves to the element's resting
state at 100% and runs with `animation-fill-mode: both`, so at 0ms the element
lands on exactly the pixel, opacity, scale and angle it occupies when nothing
is happening. Nothing is left mid-flight, invisible, or off-square. And no
motion in Relay carries information the static frame does not also carry:
possession is a hue and a mono label before it is a gesture, a published
version is a row and a pip, a breach is a colour and a number. Removing the
motion removes the reveal, not the artifact.

Adding a new animation means adding a `calc()` over `--dur-beat` — **never** a
new root duration, and **never** a `motion-reduce:` variant at a call site. The
latter is a component opting in by hand, which means the next component can
forget and nothing catches it. One such call site is on record and allowlisted
in `tests/unit/a11y-source.spec.ts` (`src/components/agency/card-tile.tsx`); it
is a round-2 defect and the fix is to delete the variant and let the token do
it.

Explicitly not present anywhere in the product: parallax, auto-playing
carousels, entrance animations on scroll, skeleton shimmer, hover lift or
scale, spinners that rotate (`Button`'s loading state is three static mono dots
plus a visually hidden label), toast slide-ins, page transitions. The full
restraint list — twelve entries, each with the reason that thing stays still —
is `MOTION.md` §5 and `FORBIDDEN_MOTION` in `src/styles/a11y-contract.ts`.

## 8. Targets, text and zoom

- **Touch targets** meet WCAG 2.5.8 (24px minimum) everywhere and the stronger
  44px on every client-facing control: `Button size="lg"` is 44px, `Field` is
  44px, the `DecisionBar` radios are padded to 44px rows, and `CardTile` is 72px
  tall.
- **Text inputs are 16px.** Below 16px, iOS Safari zooms the viewport on focus,
  which on the decision bar means the client's note field jumps out from under
  the keyboard. This is a hard floor, not a preference.
- **Reflow (1.4.10).** The product is responsive to 360px with no horizontal page
  scroll. The board is the one horizontally scrolling region above 768px, and
  below 768px it becomes a vertical stack of collapsible lanes so that even the
  board reflows.
- **Text spacing (1.4.12)** and **200% zoom (1.4.4)**: all component heights are
  content-driven (`min-h`, never fixed `h`) except `Button`, `Chip` and `Badge`,
  which are single-line by definition and are allowed to grow via line-height.
- **Language.** `<html lang>` is set by the app shell (owned by the layout, not
  by this layer) — flagged here because it is the one AA requirement the design
  layer cannot satisfy on its own.

## 9. The executable form

Everything above is prose, and prose cannot fail CI — which is why two Phase
EXIT conditions (visible focus, `prefers-reduced-motion`) stood UNPROVEN at the
end of round 1. The same floor now exists as code:

| Artifact | What it is |
|---|---|
| `src/styles/a11y-contract.ts` | The numbers as data: every contrast pair with its measured ratio, `contrastRatio()` and a colour parser that throws rather than guessing, the focus-ring expectations, the motion contract, the hostile white-label values, the locked-token list, target and reflow floors. Owned by the design layer. No dependencies. |
| `docs/design/A11Y-ASSERTIONS.md` | The Playwright and vitest specs that consume it, written out to be lifted into `tests/`. Owned, once lifted, by QA. |

Two of those suites need no running application and can go green immediately:
the contrast maths (vitest, recomputed from the shipped hexes) and the
`outline: none` source scan.

**If you change a token in `globals.css`, change `TOKENS` and the affected
`measured` values in `a11y-contract.ts` in the same commit, and recompute this
document.** The contract asserts both that a pair clears its WCAG threshold
*and* that it still measures what the design layer measured — the second
assertion is what catches a change nobody looked at.

## 10. What this layer cannot verify

Stated plainly so it is not assumed done:

- axe. `@axe-core/playwright` would be genuinely valuable and it is a new
  dependency, which needs an ADR and the Architect's approval. Everything in
  `A11Y-ASSERTIONS.md` is deliberately written without one.
- `<html lang>`, document `<title>` per route, and the skip link's target — all
  live in `src/app/layout.tsx`, which the design layer does not own.
- Screen-reader verification with an actual reader (VoiceOver / NVDA). The
  accessible names in `COMPONENTS.md` are specified, not yet heard.
